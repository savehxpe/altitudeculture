01 /// THE MOVEMENT

Lesotho's High Mountain Kingdom

Maluti Mountains. 3,000 meters above sea level. Snow in winter. Culture all year. We are building the first Culture-Sports Festival in African snow — right here at the top of Southern Africa.

This is not a pitch. This is a movement. Snow, sport, music, Basotho heritage, youth jobs, and local businesses coming together in one place: the Maluti Mountains. A festival that puts the community first and brings the world up.

01/ 04
Snow & Speed Afriski, Mahlasela Pass

Afriski — Africa's Snow Playground

height3,222M ac_unitSnow Season skiingSki + Snowboard
THE SCENE

Africa's highest ski lift at 3,222m. Snow from June to August. One of only two ski destinations in Southern Africa.

THE FEELING

Cold air, warm blood. Edge bite on firmpack. The Maluti range on fire at golden hour. Music at the base lodge.

THE FESTIVAL

This is where the Culture-Sports Festival lives. Ski races, snowboard sessions, live music, and community markets on the snow.

THE IMPACT

Local jobs for youth. Business for lodges, transport, food, and gear. A reason for the world to visit Lesotho in winter.

02/ 04
Arrival & Threshold Sani Pass, Mokhotlong Border

Sani Pass — The Gateway

height2,876M route9km, 1,332m climb celebrationHighest Pub in Africa
THE SCENE

A 4x4 road carved into the mountain. A San footpath before it was a border. The mist clears and Lesotho appears.

THE FEELINGStanding at the threshold where Basotho herders still cross on foot. KZN below. Lesotho above. The herder nods.

THE FESTIVAL

Welcome convoys, photography stops, and the highest pub in Africa. The arrival is part of the experience.

THE IMPACT

Tourism spread from the border into the mountains. Every convoy brings business to Mokhotlong, homestays, and guides.

03/ 04
Night & Sky Sehlabathebe National Park

Sehlabathebe — The Quiet

nights_stayAfrica's Darkest Skies landscape2,400M UNESCO waterfallTsoelikane Falls
THE SCENE

UNESCO-listed park at 2,400m. Bortle 1.2 — among the darkest skies on the continent. Stars in a way you have never seen.

THE FEELING

Silence. Cold air. The Milky Way reflected in a mountain tarn. No light for 50km in any direction.

THE FESTIVAL

Night hikes, astro-photography workshops, sunrise yoga at the escarpment edge. A different kind of program.

THE IMPACT

Eco-tourism that protects the park and brings visitors deeper into Lesotho. Guided hikes create direct income for communities.

04/ 04
Heritage & Journey Senqu River Valley, Maluti Mountains

Basotho Pony — The Original Way

route14 KM through villages height2,600M groupsCommunity-Run
THE SCENE

Through Senqu-fed streams. Transhumance since time immemorial. The Basotho pony — a breed shaped by altitude and history.

THE FEELING

Hooves on basalt. No engine. No GPS. The path lives in the rider's bones. Villages appear through the mist.

THE FESTIVAL

Multi-day pony treks as a festival program. Cultural evenings in villages. Storytelling, food, and music around the fire.

THE IMPACT

Direct money into remote mountain villages. Homestays, guides, pony owners, and craft sellers all benefit. Tourism that stays local.

04 /// THE PROBLEM WE SOLVE

Lesotho Has Everything. Except a Way to Share It.

Our mountains have snow, our people have culture, our youth need opportunity. The missing piece is a festival that brings it all together. That is what Altitude Culture is building.

01NEW EXPERIENCES

Making New Experiences

Transforming our mountains into active zones for skiing, snowboarding, and high-altitude festival energy. Scenery becomes activity. Visitors stay longer, spend more, and come back.

02VISIBILITY

Putting Lesotho on the Map

Using digital platforms, content creators, and festival media to tell the world that Lesotho is Africa's High Mountain Kingdom — a destination you cannot experience anywhere else.

03SERVICE

World-Class Service

Training youth as ambassadors of Basotho Hospitality. Better service means repeat visitors, and repeat visitors mean a stable economy. The quality of welcome becomes a reason to return.

04HERITAGE

Protecting Our Roots

The Basotho blanket, our history, our traditions — they are not an extra. They are the foundation. We save our culture by making it part of our future. Tourism that honors where it comes from.

05LOCAL WINNERS

Supporting Local Winners

When the festival wins, local businesses, entrepreneurs, and communities win too. 90% of the impact stays local — feeding into shops, transport, homestays, and craft. Money in the hands of the people.
